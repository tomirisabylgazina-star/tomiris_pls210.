Separating from Parents, Finding the Self

How psychological separation from parents relates to identity commitment and confidence in life choices among university students in Kazakhstan. A positivist study, 2026.

The positivist tradition

The puzzle

The old picture of growing up

  • Emerging adulthood is when students renegotiate their ties with parents and settle who they are (Koepke & Denissen, 2012).
  • A common assumption is that more independence is simply healthier.
  • But distance is not autonomy: separation can slide into detachment (Beyers et al., 2003).
Independence may begin with small decisions, such as who decides and whose values count, long before any big life choice.
My research question · 2026
Among undergraduate students at universities in Kazakhstan in 2026, does greater psychological separation from parents predict stronger identity commitment and greater confidence in their life choices, including studies and careers?
POPULATIONUndergraduates
CONTEXTKazakhstan
TIME2026
Epistemological positioning

Why positivism, specifically

AN INTERVIEW WOULD ASK

“What does becoming independent from your parents mean to you?” Rich, but it cannot show how strong the link is across students.

I’M ASKING INSTEAD

Across many students, do higher separation scores go with higher identity commitment and more confidence in life choices?

Measurable

Separation, identity and confidence all have scales.

Testable

I state a prediction and check it against data.

Standardised

Every student answers the same questions.
Literature · 1 of 2

Defining separation, linking it to identity

Defining separation

  • Separation has four parts: functional, attitudinal, emotional and conflictual independence (Hoffman, 1984).
  • Being far from parents is not the same as being autonomous (Beyers et al., 2003).
  • Emotions shape how separation unfolds in emerging adulthood (Glavač & Zupančič, 2026).

Separation and identity

  • Identity development and separation are intertwined (Koepke & Denissen, 2012).
  • Parent relations, identity and adjustment interact (Meeus et al., 2005).
  • Emotional separation and parental trust relate to identity across cultures (Sugimura et al., 2018).
  • Separation profiles differ by age and relate to identity (Sugimura et al., 2022).
Literature · 2 of 2

Development, region and context

Over time and in the region

  • Separation changes over time in late adolescence (Aslan & Gelbal, 2016).
  • Separation is linked with feeling adult and with wellbeing at ages 18-27 (Manukyan, 2022).

University life, and why identity matters

  • Parents and friends are compared as supports for university adjustment (Wider et al., 2023).
  • Identity development is linked with social-emotional difficulties (Potterton et al., 2022).
The research gap

What I still want to understand

Sources link separation and identity. But they leave room to ask whether the link holds for university students in Kazakhstan, and whether it reaches the wider choices students make about life.

Question 1

Does a student who is more independent from parents feel surer about who they are?

Question 2

Does that go with more confidence in choosing their own studies, career and life path?
Concepts and measurement

What each concept means and how I measure it

Psychological separation

Independence from parents in four ways: practical, attitudinal, emotional and conflictual.
Measure: Hoffman’s PSI (1984)

Identity commitment

How firmly a student has chosen and identifies with important life directions.
Measure: U-MICS commitment subscale (Crocetti et al., 2008)

Confidence in life choices

How sure a student is of making their own choices, including studies and careers.
Measure: Decision-confidence scale adapted from Betz et al. (1996)

Controls

Things that could explain the link.
Measure: Age · gender · year of study · living arrangement
Psychological separation · Hoffman (1984)

The four parts of separation

Functional independence

Managing practical matters, such as money, daily tasks and everyday decisions, without parents’ help.

Attitudinal independence

Holding your own beliefs, values and attitudes, even where they differ from your parents’.

Emotional independence

Not needing excessive approval, closeness or emotional support from parents.

Conflictual independence

Being free from excessive guilt, anger, resentment or mistrust toward parents.
Positivist tradition

Theory and hypotheses

Psychological separation

functional · attitudinal · emotional · conflictual
→
Identity commitment
→
Confidence in life choices (studies, careers)
Mechanism: separated students weigh options by their own values, giving firmer commitments and more confident choices.
H1: Higher separation → higher identity commitment
H2: Higher separation → higher confidence in life choices
Working expectation · open to change

What I expect to see

01 · SEPARATION

“Do I make my own decisions?”

02 · IDENTITY

“Do I know who I am?”

03 · CHOICES

“Do I trust my own choices?”
  • I expect a positive link, strongest for practical and attitudinal independence.
  • Emotional distance may not help if it becomes detachment.
  • The data may show a weaker link, or none.
Design and alternatives

Testing it, and what could compete

Design and sample

  • Cross-sectional online survey, 2026
  • Undergraduates aged 18-25, convenience sample
  • At least 100-120, aiming for 200+
  • Multiple regression, controls first
  • Kazakh and Russian versions, piloted
  • Anonymous, voluntary, informed consent

What could compete

  • Reverse direction: identity may ease separation
  • Detachment: distance is not autonomy
  • Third variables: age, year, living away
  • Limits: self-report, one time point, Western-built scales

Not graded

What I understand now, and what I still don’t

Understand now

Separation is not detachment. More distance from parents does not automatically mean more autonomy, so how I measure it matters.

Still unclear

Whether Western scales mean the same in Kazakhstan, how to measure confidence in “life choices” without being vague, and how much one survey can say about cause.
